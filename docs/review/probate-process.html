The probate process and timeline

For probate practitioners and STEP members. Everything below is what the tool actually does today, with the statutory basis it applies. If a step is out of order, a deadline is wrong, or a warning is missing, that is the feedback wanted.

The seeded checklist: 41 steps in 6 phases

A new estate is seeded with this ordered checklist. Every step becomes both a timeline step and a task, with dependency links so later steps show as blocked until their predecessors are done, and a suggested owner (family or executor).

First days

  1. Get the death verified and the MCCD issued
  2. Tell close family, friends and any employer
  3. Find the will, codicils and funeral wishes
  4. Secure the home, belongings and pets; insure the empty property
  5. Arrange the funeral

Registering the death

  1. Register the death within 5 days and order certified copies
  2. Give the certificate for burial or cremation to the funeral director
  3. Use Tell Us Once within 28 days

Notifying organisations

  1. Notify banks, building societies, NS&I and Premium Bonds
  2. Notify pension providers, including any armed forces service pension
  3. Notify insurers
  4. Notify the mortgage lender, or the landlord or housing association
  5. Notify utility suppliers
  6. Notify telecoms providers
  7. Notify TV Licensing and streaming or subscription services
  8. Notify health and care providers
  9. Notify the employer or former employer and professional bodies
  10. Notify clubs, societies and memberships
  11. Deal with digital accounts

Cancel, redirect, protect

  1. Cancel subscriptions, memberships and recurring payments
  2. Redirect post and stop marketing mail
  3. Deal with any vehicle
  4. Claim the Council Tax Class F exemption
  5. Trace forgotten assets via the free official services

Administering the estate

  1. Confirm the executors named in the will
  2. Open an executors' bank account
  3. Value the estate
  4. Assess inheritance tax and determine the reporting route
  5. Complete the IHT400 and required schedules; arrange payment
  6. Pay any inheritance tax due by the statutory deadline
  7. Submit the IHT400 and wait for the HMRC code
  8. Apply for probate and receive the grant
  9. Place the Section 27 creditor notice
  10. Collect the assets
  11. Settle debts and creditors
  12. Finalise administration-period income tax and capital gains tax
  13. Prepare estate accounts and make interim distributions
  14. Wait out the claims window, then make the final distribution
  15. Apply for the IHT30 clearance certificate and retain records

Support and wellbeing

  1. Arrange bereavement support
  2. Check bereavement-related benefits

The deadline engine: every rule it applies

Deadlines are derived by pure, unit-tested functions from the date of death and recorded events. Month arithmetic clamps to month-end (a death on 31 August gives an inheritance tax due date of 28 or 29 February).

DeadlineRule appliedStatutory basis
Inheritance tax payment dueEnd of the 6th month after the month of deathIHTA 1984 s.226(1)
IHT400 account filing dueEnd of the 12th month after the month of deathIHTA 1984 s.216(6)(a)
Section 27 claims window2 months and 1 day from the later notice date (Gazette or local paper)Trustee Act 1925 s.27(1)
Instalment option10 equal yearly instalments, first at the normal due dateIHTA 1984 s.227
CGT on residential property saleCompletion date + 60 daysFA 2019 Sch.2, as amended
ISA tax exemption endsEarlier of the 3rd anniversary of death or completion of administrationSI 1998/1870 reg.2(1), as amended
Earliest probate application after IHT400Submission + 20 working days (Monday to Friday)HMRC and HMCTS guidance

What is actually raised on an estate's deadline list: the two IHT dates always; the Section 27 date once a creditor notice is recorded; the ISA date only if an ISA asset exists; one 60-day CGT deadline per recorded disposal. The dashboard flags any past-due deadline with an overdue marker and shows the soonest five.

Known limitations, stated openly

  • The instalment schedule and the 20-working-day probate wait are computed and tested but not yet surfaced as deadline entries on the estate; they are on the list to wire in.
  • The 20-working-day rule does not model public holidays; the code says so and expects the user to check.
  • Deadline alerting is currently the dashboard's overdue flag; there is no separate advance-warning notification yet.
