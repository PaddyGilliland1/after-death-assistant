Bereavement support and tone

For bereavement advisers and counsellors. This page shows exactly who the tool signposts, the rules its language follows, and where the human boundaries sit. If the signposting is wrong or incomplete, or the tone would land badly with someone grieving, that is the feedback wanted.

The "When you need help" directory: all 17 entries

Every phone number was checked against the organisation's own website (18 July 2026); the two that could not be fully verified say "check the website" instead of guessing hours. Numbers are tap-to-dial; everything listed is free to contact.

Someone to talk to

  • Samaritans (116 123, 24 hours): someone to talk to in distress, without judgement
  • NHS urgent mental health help (111, 24 hours): urgent support via the mental health option
  • Cruse Bereavement Support (0808 808 1677): trained bereavement volunteers
  • Marie Curie Support Line (0800 090 2309): terminal illness, dying and bereavement
  • The Silver Line, run by Age UK (0800 4 70 80 90, 24 hours): conversation and support for older people
  • WAY Widowed and Young (online): peer support when a partner died at 50 or under

Practical help and money

  • Bereavement Advice Centre (0800 634 9494): practical what-to-do-after-a-death advice
  • National Bereavement Service (0800 0246 121): registration through probate, with emotional signposting
  • Tell Us Once: reports the death to most of government in one go
  • DWP Bereavement Service (0800 151 2012): benefits checks and Bereavement Support Payment
  • Citizens Advice (0800 144 8848): benefits, debt, housing, everyday legal problems
  • Age UK Advice Line (0800 678 1602, 365 days): advice for older people and their families
  • StepChange (0800 138 1111): free, confidential debt advice

Armed forces families

  • SSAFA Forcesline (0800 260 6767): serving personnel, veterans and families
  • Veterans UK (0808 1914 218): pensions, compensation and welfare

Tax and probate

  • Probate helpline, HMCTS (0300 303 0648)
  • HMRC Inheritance Tax helpline (0300 123 1072)

The seeded checklist also carries two dedicated wellbeing steps (arranging bereavement support; checking bereavement-related benefits), so support is part of the process, not a footnote.

The language rules the tool holds itself to

The assistant's tone is not left to chance; these rules are written into its instructions and the interface follows the same conventions:

  • Calm, plain UK English; short sentences and everyday words; paragraphs of three sentences or fewer.
  • Warm but not effusive, and never breezy about the death. No exclamation marks.
  • Lists over long paragraphs, because stressed people scan.
  • The assistant says plainly what the official guidance does not cover, rather than improvising; where nothing relevant exists it declines rather than guesses.
  • Estimates are always labelled as estimates when the estate's own figures are discussed.

Accessibility measures

  • WCAG 2.2 AA is the working target: labelled progress bars, status and alert roles for dynamic content, decorative icons hidden from screen readers, visually hidden data tables behind charts.
  • Phone numbers render as tap-to-dial links for someone doing all of this from a phone.

Known limitations, stated openly

  • WCAG 2.2 AA is a target the interface is built against, not an audited certification; an accessibility review would be genuinely valued.
  • The directory is England-and-Wales-shaped; advisers may know organisations that should be added, and the two unverified numbers need confirming.
